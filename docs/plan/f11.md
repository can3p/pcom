## F11 — Email subscriptions to an author's public posts

Status: running · Depends on: — · Branch: `claude/vibrant-fermat-dxl7k9`

A visitor without an account subscribes by email to an author's public posts, confirms the address from a
mail, and manages every subscription of that address from a link that every mail carries. The author opts in,
sees who subscribed, hears about every new and lost subscriber, and decides per post whether it is mailed.

Bounces and complaints are wave F12, which depends on this one.

Decided on 2026-11-05:

- **The author opts in.** A new setting, `users.email_subscriptions_enabled` (default false). A journal can
  be subscribed to only while it is open to everybody (`profile_visibility = public`) **and** the setting
  is on. Otherwise the author's subscriptions are **paused**: they are kept, nothing is mailed, the subscribe
  form is hidden, and the manage page says "@alice isn't sending posts by email right now". Settings warns
  an author who has subscribers before they switch the setting off or narrow the journal: "N people
  subscribed by email won't get your posts while …". Subscribers get no mail about it.
- **"Audience" replaces the profile-visibility wording.** The Settings section is "Audience"; the select is
  "Who can read your journal", with "Your connections" / "Anyone with a pcom account" / "Anyone on the
  internet". Under the last option, one line says what it means: "Your journal and public posts appear on
  pcom's front page and public RSS feed, and you can let people subscribe by email." The checkbox "Let
  people without an account subscribe to your public posts by email" sits right below and is enabled only
  for that option. Only labels change: the `profile_visibility` enum and its values stay.
- **The author sees the addresses.** The controls page has an "Email subscribers" section with the count and
  the list of confirmed addresses with the date each subscribed, and a Remove button on each row (red,
  asks first). A removed subscriber gets no mail, and the author gets no "lost a subscriber" mail for it.
  Pending subscriptions are neither counted nor listed. Because the author sees the address, the subscribe
  form and the confirmation page both say "@alice will see your email address", and so does the privacy
  policy.
- **The author is mailed** when a subscription is confirmed (not when it is requested, so bots can't fill
  the author's inbox) and when a subscriber unsubscribes. Both mails name the address.
- **Which posts are mailed.** Only public posts, at most once each: `posts.subscribers_mailed_at` records it,
  so neither republishing after a draft nor switching visibility back and forth mails subscribers twice. The
  post form shows one checkbox, "Email this post to N subscribers", when the post is public, the author can be
  subscribed to and has at least one subscriber, and the post was never mailed to subscribers. It is
  checked by default on a first publish and unchecked when an already published post becomes public. While
  the author can be subscribed to, the Public radio's label adds "and your N email subscribers". The API
  takes an optional `notify_subscribers`; leaving it out applies the same defaults. Mails to connections
  don't change.
- **A subscriber with a pcom account** is treated like any other address, so a connection who also
  subscribed gets both mails. A later "follow" feature (public posts of an account in the feed without a
  connection) will have its own table; nothing here anticipates it beyond the `email_` prefix of the tables.
- **`{cut}` hides the rest behind a link**, as it already does in the new post mail (`types.ViewEmail`,
  golden `new_post_rich_body`): the subscriber mail reuses `newPostBody`.
- **Subscribing needs JavaScript** (proof of work, below). There is no fallback without it.
- **One template system, a third audience.** Subscriber mails use the shared layout. `mail.Header.NoAccount`
  becomes an audience with three values (account, no account, subscriber); the subscriber footer says "You
  subscribed to @alice's public posts at pcom" with the manage link and an unsubscribe link. A subscriber's
  post mail has "Read on pcom" instead of "Read and comment", and carries `List-Unsubscribe` and
  `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` (RFC 8058), which Gmail and Yahoo require from bulk
  senders.

### Data

- `email_subscribers (id, email, email_canonical, manage_token, created_at)`: one row per address as
  entered (`pgsession.NormalizeEmail`), unique on `email`. `manage_token` is 32 random bytes, base64url,
  unique; it authorizes the manage page and every unsubscribe. `email_canonical`
  (`pgsession.CanonicalEmail`) is what the caps count by, so `+tags` and Gmail dots don't multiply them.
- `email_subscriptions (id, subscriber_id, author_id, status pending|active, confirm_token, confirm_sent_at,
  confirmed_at, created_at)`, unique on `(subscriber_id, author_id)`, deleted with the subscriber or the
  author's account. `confirm_token` as `manage_token`, unique.
- **Unsubscribing deletes the row**; a subscriber row with no subscriptions left is deleted in the same
  transaction. Pending rows older than `SUBSCRIBE_CONFIRM_LIFETIME` are deleted by a pruner, like
  unconfirmed accounts (`accounts.RunPruner`).
- `subscription_mail_log (email_canonical, kind confirm|manage_link, sent_at)`: every confirmation and
  manage-link mail, for the caps. Rows older than a day are pruned.
- `users.email_subscriptions_enabled boolean not null default false`, `posts.subscribers_mailed_at
  timestamptz null`.

### Flows

All answers to an anonymous request are the same whatever the address's state, so no page tells who
subscribes to whom.

1. **Subscribe** (`POST /form/subscribe/:username`): an email field shown to anonymous visitors on the journal
   and on a public post of an author who can be subscribed to. Logged-in users see nothing. The reply is
   always "Check your inbox to confirm". New address or new author: a pending subscription and a
   `subscriber_confirm` mail. A pending one whose confirmation is still valid: nothing is sent. An expired
   pending one: a new token and mail. An active one: nothing is sent.
2. **Confirm** (`GET /subscriptions/confirm/:token` shows a page with a Confirm button,
   `POST` confirms). GET never changes anything: mail scanners open links. Confirming an active
   subscription again changes nothing; an unknown or expired token shows "This link has expired" and the
   subscribe form's location.
3. **Manage** (`GET /subscriptions/:manage_token`): every author the address follows, paused ones marked,
   with Unsubscribe on each row and "Unsubscribe from all" (POSTs, asking first, per the interface rules).
4. **Lost the link** (`/subscriptions` with an email field): mails `subscriber_manage_link` if the address has
   subscriptions, nothing otherwise; the reply is the same.
5. **One-click unsubscribe** (`POST /subscriptions/:manage_token/unsubscribe/:subscription_id`): what
   `List-Unsubscribe-Post` posts to. It takes no CSRF token (the mail client posts it; the token in the URL
   authorizes it) and answers 200 with a short page, so it also works from a browser.

### Mails

New, each in its own file under `pkg/mail` with samples and goldens:

| Name | email_type | To | Audience |
|---|---|---|---|
| `subscriber_confirm` | `subscription_confirm` | the address | no account |
| `subscriber_manage_link` | `subscription_manage_link` | the address | no account |
| `subscriber_new_post` | `subscription_post` | each active subscriber | subscriber, with the RFC 8058 headers |
| `author_new_subscriber` | `subscription_author_new` | the author | account |
| `author_subscriber_left` | `subscription_author_left` | the author | account |

`subscriber_confirm` and `subscriber_manage_link` show nothing a visitor typed, only the author's username
and pcom's own text, so they carry no payload worth spamming. The unique id of a post mail is the post id
plus the subscription id. From name: "@alice on pcom" for `subscriber_new_post`, "Your pcom" otherwise.

### Anti-abuse

Asymmetric on purpose: the caps are by the **target address**, which an attacker can't rotate, and the
proof of work costs a reader about a second once and a bot that second on every attempt. No external
services.

- **Caps by target** (`email_canonical`): one open confirmation per address and author; at most
  `SUBSCRIBE_CONFIRMS_PER_ADDRESS` confirmation mails and `SUBSCRIBE_MANAGE_LINKS_PER_ADDRESS` manage-link
  mails per address in 24 hours. Over a cap, the reply is the same and nothing is sent.
- **Site-wide brake**: past `SUBSCRIBE_CONFIRMS_PER_HOUR` confirmation mails in the last hour, the subscribe
  and manage-link forms answer "Subscriptions are paused, try again later" and the admin gets one
  `admin_subscriptions_braked` mail per hour (account audience, `ADMIN_ADDRESS`).
- **Proof of work** (`pkg/abuse`): the form carries a challenge `{issued_at, salt, difficulty}` signed with
  HMAC-SHA256 under a key derived from `SESSION_SALT`. A Stimulus controller (`pow_controller.js`) finds a
  nonce whose SHA-256 over challenge and nonce has `SUBSCRIBE_POW_DIFFICULTY` leading zero bits, with
  `crypto.subtle`, and fills a hidden field before submit; the submit button says "Checking…" meanwhile.
  The server verifies the signature, the age (`SUBSCRIBE_POW_MAX_AGE`) and one hash, and rejects a
  challenge it has seen (a `pow_challenges_used (salt, used_at)` table pruned past the max age).
- **Honeypot and minimum fill time**: a hidden field that must stay empty, and a submit sooner than
  `SUBSCRIBE_MIN_FILL_TIME` after the challenge was issued is refused. Both refusals look like success.
- **Address checks** (disposable domains, a domain that can't receive mail) are in "Email addresses" below.

`pkg/abuse` takes no dependency on subscriptions, so #123 (signups with bot protection) can use it.

### Email addresses

`can3p/anti-disposable-email` v0.1.0 replaces the 2023 pseudo-version pcom pins. It has about 99,000
disposable domains instead of about 3,500, matches subdomains (`x@foo.mailinator.com`), reports relay
services (`duck.com`, SimpleLogin, Apple Hide My Email) apart from disposable ones, and gives a canonical
mailbox for more providers. Its README lists what it leaves to the application ("What's missing"); this is
what pcom does about each gap.

- **One importer.** A new `pkg/emailaddr` is the only package that imports the library (a `depguard` rule
  denies it elsewhere). It returns `Address{Stored, Canonical, Disposable, Relay}` and does the DNS check
  below. Signup's `CheckSignupEmail` and the subscriptions service use it.
- **Breaking changes in the upgrade don't reach pcom:** pcom is on Go 1.27 (the library needs 1.26), and
  it uses neither the removed `DisposableList` nor the `update` package.
- **Syntax stays `validation.EmailRE`; the library only classifies.** The library rejects reserved test
  domains (`.test`, used by pcom's tests in 52 files) and flags `example.com` and `example.org` as
  disposable. pcom therefore doesn't reject an address because the library can't parse it. Such an address
  is neither disposable nor a relay, as it is today. Test fixtures that go through an address check
  move from `example.com`/`example.org` to `example.test` (about 18 places in `pkg/service/accounts`,
  `pkg/forms` and `e2e`).
- **Canonical mailbox: the library's rules plus pcom's.** `pgsession.CanonicalEmail` keeps its name and
  callers. It now uses the library's `Canonical`, which also covers Outlook, Hotmail and Live, iCloud and
  Proton aliases, Yahoo's `-keyword` and Fastmail's `x@user.fastmail.com`. pcom keeps its own stricter
  rule on top: a `+tag` is removed on **every** domain. The library keeps it on custom domains, but pcom's
  "one account per mailbox" test (`+tag of an existing user` on a custom domain) depends on removing it.
  The subscription caps count by the same key. Existing `users.email_canonical` values are not
  recomputed (decided 2026-11-05): an alias of an existing account on a newly covered provider can still
  sign up, which matters little while signups are closed.
- **No DNS in the library, so pcom checks the domain.** `emailaddr.CheckDomain(ctx, domain)` uses
  `net.Resolver` with the timeout `EMAIL_MX_TIMEOUT` (default `2s`; `0` turns the check off). It refuses
  the address, with the field error "This address can't receive mail", when:
  - the domain doesn't exist;
  - it has a null MX (RFC 7505);
  - it has neither MX nor A/AAAA records.

  Any other DNS failure, or a timeout, lets the address through and logs it, so a slow resolver never
  blocks anybody. The check runs after the proof of work and the caps, so an anonymous visitor can't make
  pcom resolve names without paying for it. Tests swap the resolver with `emailaddr.WithResolver`; the E2E
  harness sets the timeout to `0`. Signup and the waiting list don't use it (decided 2026-11-05): signups
  are closed and the waiting list form is switched off in code, so it would protect nothing today.
  Reopening signups (#123) is when to add it.
- **The list is frozen at build time.** The library opens a list-update PR every month and is tagged after
  merging. pcom's dependabot (`gomod`, daily) then proposes the bump; there's no runtime refresh.
- **No allow or block lists of pcom's own.** A provider flagged by mistake goes into the library's
  `data/allowlist_local.txt` by PR, so every user of the library benefits.
- **Disposable addresses** are refused on subscribe with the field error "Please use a permanent address"
  and a log line with the domain only. There is no admin mail: bots would turn it into a flood. Signup
  keeps its `admin_throwaway_email` mail.
- **Relays are accepted on subscribe.** People who hide their address are the readers an email subscription
  suits, and the author sees the relay address, not the real one. Signup accepts relays too (decided
  2026-11-05); only disposable addresses are refused there.
