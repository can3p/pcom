## F13 — Subscribers reply to a post by email

Status: planned · Depends on: F11, F12, the open questions below · Branch: `feat/f13-subscriber-replies`

A subscriber answers a post mail (F11's `subscriber_new_post`) with their mail client's Reply. pcom
receives the reply, checks it, removes the quoted post, and forwards the text to the author in a pcom
mail. pcom doesn't receive mail today; this wave adds inbound mail with no provider types in pcom, in the
same way F12 handles bounces and complaints.

Decided on 2026-11-05 (proposed; see the open questions):

- **The author opts in separately.** A new setting, "Let subscribers reply to your posts by email"
  (`users.email_replies_enabled`, default false), sits under the subscriptions checkbox and is enabled only
  while subscriptions are. While it's off, post mails carry no reply address and nothing is forwarded.
- **Every post mail has its own reply address**, `r+<token>@<REPLY_DOMAIN>`. The token is 16 random bytes
  in base32 (26 characters, inside the 64-octet limit on the part before the `@`). It names one
  subscription and one post, so the author knows which post the reply is about. It is a row in
  `subscription_post_mails (token, subscription_id, post_id, created_at)`, written by the F11 fan-out. The
  token authorizes nothing but replying; the manage token never appears in a reply address. A row older
  than `REPLY_TOKEN_LIFETIME` stops accepting replies.
- **The subscriber is told** in the post mail's footer: "Reply to this mail to write to @alice; @alice will
  see your address."
- **Only text is forwarded.** pcom forwards the plain-text part, or the HTML part turned into text when
  there is no plain part. The quoted post and the signature are removed. Attachments are dropped, and the
  author's mail says so. Text over `REPLY_MAX_LENGTH` characters is cut, with a note.
- **Nothing is stored but a log row.** `subscriber_replies (subscription_id, post_id, received_at, outcome,
  drop_reason)` holds no content. It is there for the caps and for the operator to see why a reply was
  dropped. The text exists only in the queued mail to the author.
- **A dropped reply is dropped silently.** The subscriber gets no automatic answer, because the sender of a
  dropped reply may be forged (backscatter). The log row records why.
- **The author's mail**, `author_subscriber_reply`, comes from "Your pcom". Its subject is "Re: <post
  subject>". The reply sits in the quote box under "<address> replied to your post", with a link to the
  post. Reply-To follows Q-A.

### Checks on an incoming reply, in order

1. **Webhook authorization**: HTTP basic auth against `MAIL_INBOUND_SECRET`, and a body cap
   (`MAIL_INBOUND_MAX_BYTES`).
2. **Automatic mail is dropped:**
   - `Auto-Submitted` other than `no`;
   - `Precedence: bulk`, `junk`, `list` or `auto_reply`;
   - `X-Autoreply` or `X-Autorespond`;
   - an empty envelope sender or `MAILER-DAEMON`;
   - a `multipart/report` (delivery or read reports).

   pcom's own post mails get `Auto-Submitted: auto-generated`, so a well-behaved autoresponder doesn't
   answer them at all.
3. **The token** exists and isn't past `REPLY_TOKEN_LIFETIME`; its subscription is active; its author can
   be subscribed to and has replies on.
4. **The sender is the subscriber**: the `From` address's canonical mailbox (`pgsession.CanonicalEmail`)
   equals the subscriber's, and the provider's authentication results pass as Q-C decides. A reply
   forwarded from another address, or a forged `From`, is dropped.
5. **Caps**: at most `REPLIES_PER_SUBSCRIPTION_DAY` per subscription and `REPLIES_PER_AUTHOR_DAY` per
   author. Past either, the reply is dropped.
6. **Content**: the extracted text must not be empty once the quote is removed. If removing the quote
   would leave nothing, the whole text is kept instead.

### Removing the quoted post

This is a heuristic, in `pkg/emailreply` (pure functions, golden tests):
- **In the plain-text part**, the reply is cut at the first of:
  - an attribution line followed by `>` lines ("On … wrote:", and the common localized forms the tests
    list);
  - a run of `>` lines that reaches the end;
  - `-----Original Message-----`;
  - an Outlook header block (`From:` … `Sent:` … `To:` … `Subject:`).

  A signature after a line that is exactly `-- ` is removed.
- **In the HTML part**, the quote elements (`div.gmail_quote`, `blockquote[type=cite]`,
  `div#appendonsend` and the Outlook divider) are removed before the HTML is turned into text with
  `github.com/inbucket/html2text`, which is already in `go.sum` as an indirect dependency.

Goldens use real replies from Gmail, Apple Mail, Outlook, Thunderbird and Fastmail, each with the
personal details replaced.

### Inbound mail without provider types

- **In gogo** (like F12's `feedback` package), an `inbound` package defines
  `inbound.Message{EnvelopeFrom, From, To []string, Subject, Text, HTML, Headers, Attachments int,
  AuthResults}` and `inbound.Parser` (`ParseWebhook(*http.Request) (*Message, error)`). A parser per
  provider lives next to its sender; the first is Mailjet's Parse API (Q-B).
- **In pcom**, `POST /hooks/mail-inbound` sits outside sessions and CSRF. The parser is chosen by
  `MAIL_INBOUND_PROVIDER`. The route answers 200 to any authorized request, so the provider doesn't retry
  a reply pcom drops on purpose; it answers 5xx only when the database fails.
- **DNS**: `REPLY_DOMAIN` (empty turns the feature off) is a subdomain such as `reply.pcom.example`. Its MX
  record points at the provider's inbound host, so the main domain's mail is untouched. `docs/running.md`
  describes the setup.
- **Changing provider** means one parser in gogo, a new MX record and one setting.

### Effects on other waves

- **F11**: the fan-out writes a `subscription_post_mails` row and sets `Reply-To` when the author has
  replies on. All subscriber mails get `Auto-Submitted: auto-generated`. TG's `Headers` carries both.
- **F12**: a complaint about `author_subscriber_reply` comes from the **author**. It must not suppress the
  author's address; it turns off replies from that subscription (`email_subscriptions.replies_blocked_at`),
  and the controls list shows that.

### Settings

`REPLY_DOMAIN`, `REPLY_TOKEN_LIFETIME` (`2160h`, 90 days), `REPLY_MAX_LENGTH` (`10000`),
`REPLIES_PER_SUBSCRIPTION_DAY` (`5`), `REPLIES_PER_AUTHOR_DAY` (`100`), `MAIL_INBOUND_PROVIDER`,
`MAIL_INBOUND_SECRET` (`settings.Secret`) and `MAIL_INBOUND_MAX_BYTES` (`1048576`). Each has a row in
`pkg/config`'s table test and appears in the `web serve --help` golden.

### Open questions

- **Q-A. Can the author answer a subscriber, and from which address?** Options:
  - (a) Reply-To is the subscriber's address, so the author answers from their own mailbox. Simple, but
    the subscriber then sees the author's address. The Settings checkbox says so.
  - (b) No Reply-To: the author reads replies but can't answer through pcom.
  - (c) Two-way relay: the author's answer also goes through pcom, so neither side sees the other's
    address. This roughly doubles the wave: author tokens, a second mail and loop protection.

  Recommended: (a) now, (c) later if authors ask for it.
- **Q-B. Which provider receives mail?** Mailjet's Parse API is the natural first choice, since pcom
  already sends through Mailjet. Mailjet's docs couldn't be read from the session that wrote this plan, so
  TV verifies:
  - a catch-all route on our own subdomain;
  - which authentication results the webhook carries;
  - the size limit;
  - retries;
  - whether the plan includes the Parse API.

  If it falls short, other options are a provider with inbound parsing (Postmark, Mailgun, SendGrid) or
  Cloudflare Email Routing with a Worker that posts to the webhook.
- **Q-C. How strictly is the sender checked?** Options:
  - (a) Require DMARC to pass, or SPF or DKIM to pass for the `From` domain. A reply without authentication
    results is dropped.
  - (b) Use authentication results when the provider sends them; otherwise accept a matching `From`.

  Recommended: (a), if TV finds the provider sends the results; otherwise the owner chooses between (b)
  and another provider.
- **Q-D. Does the subscriber get a "your reply was forwarded" mail?** Recommended: no. It could be sent to
  a forged sender, and the author's own answer (Q-A) is the confirmation.
